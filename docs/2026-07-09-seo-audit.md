## Executive summary

The June plan's basics are done and done well: keyword title tag, 31 built pages, JSON-LD on everything, GSC/Bing verified, robots/sitemap/canonicals live, scheduled-publish pipeline. This audit found the **next layer** — and it matters more than what's left of the old checklist:

1. **Two integrity problems that can get the site penalized or distrusted:** a fabricated `aggregateRating` (4.8★, 142 ratings) on a pre-launch product, and 8 live blog posts whose schema claims *future* publish dates (Sep–Nov 2026). Both are the exact patterns Google's spam systems and manual reviewers look for.
2. **A crawl bug that is still live despite being "fixed":** `www.getskoolit.com` returns **403 to everyone, including Googlebot** (verified today). The `_redirects` rule shipped in PR #28, but the www custom domain was never registered in Cloudflare Pages, so the rule never runs.
3. **Self-inflicted keyword cannibalization** on the two highest-value commercial clusters (Cozi, Skylight): the `/vs/*` and `/*-alternative/` pages title-target the same queries against each other.
4. **The SERP has moved.** Every page-one result for "Cozi alternative" and "app that reads school emails" is a *competitor-owned blog* (Sense, Nori, Calendara, Maple, FamilyHero, plus new entrants Nestify, FamilyHQ, TextConcierge, NUET, Peggy, Kinmory, Family Sync). Skoolit will not out-domain them with on-page work alone — the leverage is now (a) a citable data asset, (b) presence in the third-party roundups that both Google and LLMs actually cite, and (c) entity/LLM grounding infrastructure that none of these small competitors have built.

Good news verified today: **GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, and Bingbot all get HTTP 200** from the apex domain — no bot-blocking problem. And the homepage ranks #1 for its own brand query.

## Findings

### A. Trust & integrity (fix before anything else)

**A1. Fabricated review markup — manual-action risk.**
`public/index.html` ships `aggregateRating: 4.8, ratingCount: 142` inside the SoftwareApplication schema. The product is pre-launch; there are no 142 ratings anywhere. Fake review markup is one of the few structured-data offenses Google issues manual actions for ("Spammy structured markup"), and a manual action on a 3-month-old domain would be close to fatal. LLM answer engines also cross-check claims: an assistant asked "is Skoolit legit?" that finds a 4.8★ claim with zero App Store presence learns to distrust the domain. Remove the block entirely until real reviews exist (then mark up only where the reviews are actually displayed).

**A2. Future `datePublished` on live pages.**
8 indexable posts claim publish dates of 2026-09-15 → 2026-11-19 (the schema was written for the original gated schedule, then PR #27 released the pages early without updating dates). Google treats implausible dates as unreliable and may ignore *all* date signals from the site; freshness is a real ranking input for "2026" queries. Set `datePublished` to the actual go-live date (July 3, per PR #27 merge), add `dateModified`, and update the visible bylines to match — visible date and schema date must agree.

**A3. www.getskoolit.com still returns 403 (verified live today).**
GSC already reported this; PR #28 added the `_redirects` rule, but as the rule's own comment warns, it cannot fire until `www.getskoolit.com` is added as a custom domain on the Pages project. Nobody did the dashboard step. Every external link that uses www (people type it) currently dead-ends for users *and* crawlers, and Google keeps re-crawling a 403 host. Five-minute dashboard fix: Pages → skoolit-landing → Custom domains → add `www.getskoolit.com`, then "Validate fix" in GSC.

### B. Indexation & crawl plumbing

**B1. Noindexed URLs submitted in the sitemap.**
`/blog/7-apps-one-school/` and `/blog/why-we-built-skoolit/` are still noindex-gated (launch-day posts) but present in the submitted sitemap → GSC "Submitted URL marked 'noindex'" errors, which erode sitemap trust. Remove both from `sitemap.xml`; `scripts/publish.py` re-adds them automatically on their publish date (it only adds when absent — no code change needed).

**B2. /prototype/ is robots-blocked AND noindexed — the two directives cancel out.**
robots.txt `Disallow: /prototype/` prevents Googlebot from ever *seeing* the `noindex` meta on `/prototype/index.html`. Because the footer of every page publicly links to `/prototype/`, Google can still index the bare URL ("indexed, though blocked by robots.txt") — with your password-gate page as the snippet. Correct pattern: **remove the Disallow rule, keep the noindex meta.** Also: `/prototype/app.html` has *no* robots meta at all — add `noindex,nofollow` there before unblocking.

**B3. Sitemap carries `changefreq`/`priority` (ignored by Google) but no `lastmod` (the only field Google uses).**
Add real `lastmod` per URL and emit it from `publish.py`. `lastmod` is also what tells Bing/IndexNow-driven crawlers to re-fetch — this matters for LLM grounding freshness (ChatGPT browses via Bing's index).

**B4. 404 page has a canonical to the homepage.**
A canonical on an error page tells Google "this 404 is the homepage" — a classic soft-404 confuser. Remove the canonical from `404.html` (the `noindex` already there is correct).

### C. On-page & architecture

**C1. Keyword cannibalization on the two most valuable commercial clusters.**
- **Skylight:** `/vs/skylight/` is titled "Skylight Calendar Alternative 2026 — Skoolit vs Skylight" and `/skylight-calendar-alternative/` is "Skylight Calendar Alternative — No $300 Device Required". Two pages, same query, one domain → Google picks one arbitrarily, splits clicks and links, and often ranks *neither* well.
- **Cozi:** `/vs/cozi/` is titled "Skoolit vs Cozi: Best Cozi Alternative 2026" with H1 "The best Cozi alternative for school-heavy families" — colliding with `/cozi-alternative/`.

Fix by intent-splitting, not deleting: `/vs/*` pages own the branded head-to-head query ("skoolit vs cozi", "cozi vs skoolit") — retitle them "Skoolit vs Cozi: Feature-by-Feature (2026)" style, with zero "alternative" phrasing. `/*-alternative/` pages exclusively own "\<brand\> alternative". Then cross-link each pair one way with exact-match anchors ("looking for a Cozi alternative?" → alternative page; "detailed Skoolit vs Cozi comparison" → vs page). The Sense pair is already split correctly — copy that pattern.

**C2. Homepage H1 is still the keyword-free brand tagline.**
"Less inbox. More parenting." — flagged in the June 18 plan, still unfixed, and it's the single highest-weight on-page element on the site's highest-authority page. The plan's Option A (Matt-compatible) still stands: keep the tagline as the visual eyebrow, make the H1 the keyword line, e.g. "The school & activity email organizer that builds your family calendar for you." Note the *title tag* already targets this — the H1 just needs to agree with it.

**C3. `/vs/cozi/` is thin (≈660 words) for a SERP where the standard is 1,800-word "7 tested" pages.**
Every competitor page ranking for Cozi-adjacent queries is a long, tabled, screenshot-heavy comparison. Bring `/vs/cozi/` to parity with `/cozi-alternative/` (1,841 words): add the feature table rows only Skoolit can claim (per-child routing, urgency scoring, caregiver relay, no-forwarding inbox monitoring), pricing math (Cozi Gold $39/yr vs Skoolit), and a migration section ("what happens to your Cozi calendar when you switch").

**C4. Social preview images half-broken.**
15 of 35 pages — **including the homepage** — still point `og:image` at `assets/og.svg`. Facebook, LinkedIn, Slack, iMessage and X do not render SVG OG images; shares of the homepage currently show no image at all. PR #28 fixed 20 pages and missed these. Point everything at `og.png` (exists, 15KB), add `og:image:width`/`og:image:height`, and give the 5 money pages unique 1200×630 images (the Katie art assets can be cropped/reused).

**C5. Blog heroes are ~2.1MB PNGs — an LCP disaster on mobile.**
Every post eager-loads a 2MB+ PNG hero with no `width`/`height` (layout shift) and no `srcset`. 42MB of blog images total. Mobile LCP on 4G will be well past the 2.5s "good" threshold, and CWV is a confirmed (small) ranking input while these are exactly the pages that must rank. Convert to WebP at ≤200KB, set explicit dimensions, `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero, `loading="lazy"` on everything below the fold. One scripted pass (`cwebp -q 80` + sed) fixes all 13 posts.

**C6. Fonts: 36 pages pull render-blocking CSS from fonts.googleapis.com; the two woff2 files in `/fonts/` are used by zero pages.**
Self-host subset woff2 for Inter + Plus Jakarta Sans with `font-display: swap`, preload the two main weights, delete the unused BricolageGrotesque/HankenGrotesk files. Removes a third-party connection from the critical path of every page (~300–500ms on mobile first visit).

### D. Authority — who, how, and which sites

Generic "get backlinks" is banned from this document, so here is the named plan. The unit of work is not "a backlink," it's **getting Skoolit into the specific documents that Google ranks and LLMs quote for our queries.**

**D1. The linkable asset: turn the 94-email stat into "The School Email Load Index."**
`/blog/school-email-overload/` already contains original research (one family, 94 emails/30 days). One family is an anecdote; 25 families is a *dataset* — and datasets get cited. Recruit 25–40 waitlist parents (there is a waitlist and an email pipeline already) to run the same 30-day count via a Google Form; publish a standalone page — `/research/school-email-load-index/` — with methodology, mean/median emails per child per week, % requiring action, and one headline stat ("the average two-kid family receives N school emails per month; parents miss X%"). This is the asset every journalist, roundup writer, and LLM will cite instead of a marketing blog post. Refresh annually ("2027 Index") for compounding links.

**D2. Get into the third-party roundups that already rank (and that LLMs quote).**
Verified today as ranking page-one for our money queries — these are the pitch targets, with the ask being *inclusion in an existing list*, which is a much easier yes than a new article:
- **Mommy Poppins** — "Best Shared Family Calendar Apps (Tested by a Real Parent)" (mommypoppins.com). Pitch the author a review unit + the Load Index data.
- **Care.com editorial** — "The Best Family Calendar Apps" (care.com/c/best-calendar-organizer-apps-families). Note: Cozi is owned by care.com's founder's circle — pitch on the school-email angle they don't cover.
- **SlashGear** ("14 Alternatives to the Skylight Calendar") and **BGR** ("10 Best Alternatives to the Skylight Calendar") — both are updateable listicles at tech pubs that take PR pitches; Skoolit's "no $300 hardware" angle fits their frame exactly.
- **Cybernews** ("Best Skylight Calendar Alternatives 2026: My Top 12") — reviews-desk pitch.
- **Top10.com** family-organizer category and **Forbes Vetted** "Best Digital Calendars" — affiliate-driven; offer a trial + affiliate/ambassador terms (the ambassador program already exists at `/ambassador/`).
- **Techlicious / Cool Mom Tech / PTO Today** — parent-tech desks that do back-to-school app roundups every August; pitch in July, which is when those pieces get written.
- **Motherly** — warm channel (Lizzy, Jun 25 meeting). Follow up with the Load Index as the hook, not the product.

**D3. Journalist-request platforms — HARO is dead; use its successors.**
Sign up on **Qwoted**, **Featured.com**, and **SOS (Source of Sources, Peter Shankman's HARO successor)** as "founder, parenting-tech / former default parent" expert. August is peak back-to-school editorial season: reporters at CNBC Make It, Parents.com, Romper, and local TV all run "school communication overload" stories and need a quotable founder + a stat. 15 min/day of query-answering during August; each placement is a DR 70–90 link.

**D4. The PTA/PTO flywheel (Leto proved it works).**
PTO Today (ptotoday.com) has a vendor directory + expo circuit. Separately: offer 10 PTAs a free year for the whole board in exchange for a mention on the PTA's "resources for parents" page — school and district PTA pages live on high-trust .org/.k12 domains that pass real authority and are exactly the "local, human-vetted" sources LLMs weight for parent recommendations. The `/ambassador/` infrastructure can track it.

**D5. Pre-launch directory layer — cheap, high-leverage, and LLM-facing.**
- **AlternativeTo.net**: create the Skoolit listing and position it as an alternative to Cozi, Skylight, Sense, and FamilyWall. AlternativeTo is one of the most-cited sources in LLM answers for every "X alternative" query — this is the single highest-ROI LLM-visibility action available today, and it's free.
- **Product Hunt "Coming Soon" page** now (not just the Sept 15 launch) — collects followers pre-launch and creates the entity page early.
- **BetaList** — accepts pre-launch startups; DR ~80 link plus early-adopter signups.
- **Crunchbase profile + LinkedIn company page** — these two are the backbone of LLM entity resolution ("what is Skoolit?"). Currently the brand SERP is polluted by skool.com; these profiles are how both Google and LLMs disambiguate the entity.

### E. LLM-specific visibility

**E1. Crawler access is good — protect it.** GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot verified 200 today. Do not enable Cloudflare's "Block AI bots" toggle (it's one click away from wiping LLM visibility, and Bot Fight Mode caused exactly this class of problem in June).

**E2. Add `llms.txt` + `llms-full.txt`.** Emerging but zero-cost standard already read by several answer engines: a markdown summary of what Skoolit is, the canonical one-liners vs each competitor (they exist in landscape.md), pricing, and FAQ answers. The comparison one-liners are exactly the sentence-length claims LLMs lift into answers — publishing them in a machine-readable file makes Skoolit's framing the default framing.

**E3. Entity hardening: add `sameAs` to the Organization schema** (LinkedIn, Crunchbase, X, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo once created). Zero `sameAs` links exist today, and the skool.com name collision makes entity disambiguation genuinely hard for retrieval systems. This plus D5 is the fix.

**E4. Named human author (E-E-A-T + AI-content suspicion).**
Every post is bylined "By the Skoolit Team" with `author: Organization`. First-person research content ("we counted every email") from an anonymous org is the signature shape of AI-generated content farms — for both Google's helpful-content classifiers and LLM source-quality heuristics. Create one real author page (`/about/` with Laurent's name, photo, "parent of N, built Skoolit because…", LinkedIn `sameAs`), switch Article schema to `author: Person`, and byline every post. One hour of work, applies to all 14 posts.

**E5. IndexNow via Cloudflare Crawler Hints.**
One dashboard toggle (Cloudflare → Caching → Crawler Hints) pings IndexNow on every deploy → Bing indexes new pages in hours instead of weeks. ChatGPT's browsing and Copilot ground on Bing's index, so this directly shortens the pipeline from "published" to "citable by ChatGPT."
